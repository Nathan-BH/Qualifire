How it works

Same road. New meaning.

Press START at the door. From there, the app quietly splits your ride into sectors and times each one against your own recent history — no setup, nothing to configure mid-ride.

1

Ride your normal route

Morning, evening, weekend errand — whichever route you already ride. Nothing about the ride changes. The GPS trace is the same one your phone always drew.

2

Invisible gates split it into sectors

Lines drawn perpendicular across your path, downstream of junctions, never at stop lines. You never see them. You only feel the moment one falls behind you.

3

Every sector is timed against your last 10

Not a personal record from some mythical perfect ride — the rolling window of your last ten laps on that exact stretch. Recent you, not peak you.

Earned colour

How today's ride gets its colour.

Every sector is measured against a comparison window: your last 10 rides on that exact track.

Purple

Beats the best of the window — the fastest this sector has gone across your last ten attempts.

Green

Above your recent average. A genuinely good lap — better than what you've been doing lately, without needing to be a lifetime best.

Yellow

Below your recent average — styled as "time posted," not failure. This is the app's default temperature. Most laps are yellow. That's fine.

Colour only shows once there's enough history to trust it — a result should never come down to luck-of-the-GPS. Fewer than five comparable rides on a track and the sector simply stays plain ink, no colour yet, until there's enough to compare against.

QUALIFIRE

Qualifire is a personal project. Your own routes, your own history, zero servers. It runs off a single archive of one person's own commute data and exists to make an ordinary ride worth riding again — nothing more.

Not available on any app store. Not a company. Not for sale. This page is a mock, built to show what the app looks and feels like, not to sell it to anyone but its one user.
