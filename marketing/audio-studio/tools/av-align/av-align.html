av-align: audio to video alignment

What this tool will and will not do

Will:

  • Open any mp4 the browser can decode (all Qualifire renders: h264, yuv420p, 30 fps) and any wav or mp3.
  • Step one frame forward or back (33.33 ms at 30 fps), step 10 frames, jump to a time or a frame, and show time in seconds (3 decimals) and the frame number (0-based, frame 0 = 0.000 s).
  • Draw the audio waveform on the video clock, shifted by the offset, with a playhead, frame ticks when zoomed in, and the audio attacks (onsets) marked and listed in both audio time and video time.
  • Let you place the same audio file more than once (the ride uses two placements, T1 and T2), nudge the selected placement by 1 frame, 10 ms or 100 ms, and type an exact value.
  • Play video and audio together at the chosen offset (real time, Web Audio clock), and scrub: while stepping frames, play exactly the 33.33 ms of audio that belongs to the frame on screen. Scrub is exact by construction and is the mode to trust.
  • Save copy: download a copy of this whole page with the current offsets, markers and file names written into the config block; the rest of the file is unchanged.

Will not:

  • Change any file in the repo, any render, any WAV, or any brief. It is a viewer. A number you settle on becomes a change only when the coordinator re-runs the relevant build with it.
  • Guarantee sample-exact real-time sync. Browser video and Web Audio run on different clocks; the page re-syncs whenever they drift more than about 25 ms, so real-time playback is right to about 1 frame (33 ms) on a wired output, and worse on Bluetooth (100 to 200 ms of latency the page cannot see). Judge with scrub and the onset markers; use real-time playback for the feel.
  • Mix, fade, apply gain curves, or reproduce the E5 pulse layer. To hear the built mix, load the built master WAV with one placement at 0.0.
  • Know where your files are. Browsers expose only the file name of a picked file, so a saved copy records names, not paths.
  • Work in Firefox as intended (no requestVideoFrameCallback; it falls back to a coarser clock and says so). Chrome and Edge on Windows are the targets.

A placement offset "at" is the video time (seconds on the video clock, 0 = first frame) at which the audio file t = 0 is placed. Positive means the audio starts later than the video.
