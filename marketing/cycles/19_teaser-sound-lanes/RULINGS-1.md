## Rulings

### 1. The clipping STOP — decision: the kit WAVs become 32-bit float, written by prep_kit.py; the tolerance stays 4 / 1.5 LSB (no loosening)

1.1 Why not (B) loosen the tolerance: 642 LSB is 2.0 % of full scale. A check that accepts 2 % cannot see a wrong gain of 2 %, a fade off by a few hundred samples, or a one-sample placement error on a transient. Check (a) exists to prove "default state = today's teaser"; it must stay tight. Audibly the 16-bit clipping is negligible at gain 0.45 (583 bed samples soft-clipped by at most 0.37 dB), but lane A ("other A", peak 1.1309) would clip in the tool at any gain above 0.88 while the build script does not, so what Nathan hears would drift from what he gets exactly where he is most likely to experiment.

1.2 Why not (C) headroom scaling: a manifest field, a compensation in the page (gain AND waveform normalisation), in verify_default_mix.py, in the tests and in the copy-list rule (gains must stay in source terms) — five places to keep consistent, one bit of resolution lost, for the same result as (A).

1.3 (A), precisely: the kit WAVs carry the float32 samples of `ride_tunetank.decode_stereo` (the build scripts' own decode) untouched, so the tool's audio path is bit-identical to the build scripts' input; the only quantisation left is the final int16 of the master, which verify (a) reproduces. Costs: disk 21 MB -> 42 MB for the nine WAVs (kit about 55 MB with the video; still one `device_stage_files` call under 60 MB); decoded RAM unchanged (Web Audio decodes to float32 anyway); Chromium's decodeAudioData reads IEEE-float WAV (format tag 3) — this is CONFIRMED by a new `data-peak` check in e2e-real (fix step 6), not assumed. Because ffmpeg's WAV muxer writes the EXTENSIBLE header and Python's `wave` cannot read it, prep_kit.py writes the WAV itself: a 12-line `write_wav_f32` (RIFF, `fmt ` 16 bytes, format tag 3, 2 ch, 44100, 32 bits, `data`), and a small RIFF reader replaces `wave` for the kit files. All nine kit WAVs use this format (e5.wav too, for uniformity; the master-reproduction check in step 6 uses the float arrays and is unchanged).

1.4 Tolerance and expected numbers: TOL_MAX 4, TOL_RMS 1.5 stay as the brief wrote them. Expected after the fix: (a) max |diff| <= 1 LSB, 0 samples > 2 LSB, rms(diff) <= 0.5 LSB, lag 0. Reason: kit floats == build floats bit-for-bit (verified in the VM: `-f f32le` raw decode and the float WAV samples are identical); verify's per-sample fade `min(1,(end-t)/fade)` equals `linspace(1,0,n,endpoint=False)` sample for sample; both sides quantise with `(clip(x,-1,1)*32767).astype(int16)`; float32 storage of e5 adds <= 6e-8 FS = 0.002 LSB. Only float64 summation order remains, which can flip one rounding at a boundary: hence <= 1 LSB (the executor's own control with the float decode gave exactly max 1 LSB, rms 0.376). (b) stays <= 2 LSB (last run: 2). (c) 0 non-zero samples. Anything above these expectations but within 4 / 1.5 passes the script; it must still be written into the report. Anything above 4 / 1.5 is a real STOP.

## Decisions taken for Nathan (plain words)
- The sound files in the tool's kit are now stored as 32-bit float instead of 16-bit. Reason: the music mp3 decodes with a few hundred samples slightly above full scale; the build scripts keep them (they only clip the final mix), a 16-bit copy would flatten them before the volume is applied, so what you hear in the tool would differ a little from what the script builds. Float keeps it identical; the kit is about 55 MB instead of 33 MB, still rebuilt by one command and never committed.
- The default clip count is 16 (the brief said 19 by a counting slip); the copy list is 17 lines.
- Times print with 3 decimals rounded half-up, so the 15.0465 s bed prints as 15.047 (that means "the whole file").
- Where two clips of one lane are under the playhead, the lane header shows both source times, left to right in the order the clips start; the copy list has one line per clip, so nothing is hidden.
- Two small display fixes: the "in"/"out" tags no longer collide where clips overlap; the frame numbers under the editor fields go from 10 to 11 px.
