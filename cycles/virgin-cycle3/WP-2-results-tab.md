## 3. The design decisions

### 3.1 A sixth top-level tab, `'results'`, in RESULT's old slot

Nathan said "re-introduce the results tab", the old RESULT *was* a top-level tab, and the bar's sideways-scroll behaviour was ruled by him **for six tabs**. Folding it into RIDES was considered and rejected: RIDES is a chronological log of individual rides — including free rides that belong to no way — and its rows open a *ride*; RESULTS is a per-way view whose rows open a *way*. Different subject, different sort, different detail. **Decision:** `Tab` gains `'results'`; `App.tsx:202` array becomes `['record','rides','routes','results','settings','demo']` — the exact old order (`0bb025d^`). The label renders as the raw string `results`, like every other tab.

### 3.2 The board: ALL rides on the way, fastest first, no tier colours — provisional (Q1: "build it and refine later")

The `STATE.md:99-100` window rule exists to make a **verdict** honest: a ride's tier colours and its "P_n of 10" line are judged against a fixed, recent, same-size field. That is a *scoring* rule. The board makes no verdict: it lists what happened, fastest first, over the rider's whole record — what Nathan asked for ("ranking tower with all the rides") and what he confirmed in Q3 ("the tower shows absolute ranking").

**Decision: the RESULTS board lists ALL stored rides on the way, fastest first, with all-time positions 1..N — and it never colours a time by tier.** Concretely:

- Positions are all-time (`tower()` from `results.ts`, unbounded). The caption above the board says so in words: `ALL 27 RIDES · fastest first`. A position on this board is a *fact about the list*, not a verdict about a ride; the ride detail's "P3 of 10" stays exactly as it is.
- **No green/yellow/purple tier colours on the board's times.** Re-judging 27 rides against each other would be a new global scoring model. Times are ink (`t.text`). The only colour is the all-time PB marker ● (D-007, `allTimeBestLapS`) — already an all-time claim everywhere in the app.
- Rides `ranks()` excludes (rider-ignored, tripwire-demoted) sit at the bottom under a dim `not ranked` sub-caption with their time and no position. Estimated/missed laps are listed there too, time shown as `NO TIME`.
- `STATE.md` gets a one-clause scoping amendment when this lands (coordinator's file, not the executor's): *"…never a global ranking, never 'of 11' — for scoring and the ride detail's rank line. The RESULTS tab's all-time list is a history view, not a scoring pool, and shows no tier colours."*
- **Provisional.** Nathan's Q1 answer is "not clear for me now, let's build it and refine later" — build exactly this; a later cycle may window the board. No escape hatch is built for that.

The `TimingTower` component is **not** reused: its anatomy (TODAY row, slot-in, 8-row clip, pre-scroll) is the post-ride board's. A new plain `HistoryBoard` renders all rows inside the page's own `ScrollView` (no nested scroll), reusing the tower's row proportions (`PAST_H` 38 px, `P# · time · gap · date`) and `towerDate()`.

### 3.3 The scatterplot's window: the last 9 ranked rides — `ghostsFor(wayId)`'s shape, drawn in time

Nathan (Q3): *"keep it at just the last 9 rides for the scatterplot instead of all rides, this way it mirrors the ranking tower."* 9 is `WINDOW_PREV` — the field the NEXT ride on this way will be judged against (`colourModel.ts:64-66` `ghostsFor(wayId)` with no exclusion: "a live lap not yet stored needs no exclusion and gets the same 9"). That gives the plot a precise meaning, distinct from the board: **the board is the all-time record; the plot is the current comparison field, laid out in time.** Beat the purple dot on your next ride → purple verdict; beat the dotted line → green; else yellow. The purple dot's age tells you how long the bar has stood, and its position from the left tells you in how many rides it drops out — exactly Nathan's "you can see how long ago the purple lap was … and you know it will disappear in X rides."

- **Window** W = `results.filter(ranks)`, ascending `startedAtMs`, `.slice(-WINDOW_PREV)` — computed in the pure model over `storedResultsForWay(wayId)` (the model takes an array; it does not call `ghostsFor`, which reads the store, but it MUST use the same `ranks()` filter and the same `WINDOW_PREV` constant imported from `colourModel.ts`, so the two can never drift). Virgin build: identical set to `ghostsFor`. Nathan's dev build: `ghostsFor` also sees archive ghosts (`shippedResults()`); the plot does not (§3.8's archive note) — stated, accepted.
- **Only ranked rides are points.** Rider-ignored, tripwire-demoted and `NO TIME` rides are not in the field, so they are not on the plot; they are on the board under `not ranked`. (The original design's hollow points are gone with the all-time plot.)
- **Not windowed to "now"**: the x range is the window's own oldest→newest ride. If the newest ride is three months old, the plot still fills the width; the tick labels say when.
- **The most recent ride is not specially marked.** The tower marks TODAY because the post-ride board is about the ride just ridden; a history view has no "today", and the right-most dot is the newest by construction. The selection ring (§3.6) is the only per-point highlight. Decided, not left open.

### 3.4 Y-axis: faster = UP; fixed fit of the window ∪ mean; minimum span; no outlier fence

- **Orientation (Q3): faster is higher.** `y(timeS) = (timeS − yMin) / (yMax − yMin) · plotH` from the top of the plot — the smallest time sits at the top, the purple dot is the **highest** point, the y-gutter labels read fastest at the top, slowest at the bottom.
- **Domain**: `lo = min(W ∪ {mean})`, `hi = max(W ∪ {mean})`, `span = hi − lo`. **Minimum span** `minSpan = max(30 s, 0.05 · median(W))` — three rides within 4 s of each other must not fill the plot with 4 seconds of drama; expand symmetrically about the midpoint when `span < minSpan`. Then **pad 10 %** of the (possibly expanded) span on each side: `yMin = lo − 0.1·span`, `yMax = hi + 0.1·span`. Nothing is ever off-scale.
- **Average** = arithmetic mean of W (mean, not median — the tier model judges against the mean of rides on record, `STATE.md:97-98`, and `tierFor` uses `stats().mean`). This one number is BOTH the dotted line and the green/yellow threshold (§3.6). It is the mean of the 9-window, never all-time.
- **Tukey fence / off-scale markers: DROPPED.** They existed to stop one puncture day squashing years of history. With a bounded window of ≤ 9 the worst case is one slow ride compressing the other eight into the upper part of the plot for at most nine more rides, after which it rolls out on its own; the rider's existing remedy for a run that should not drag the average — "Ignore in ranking" in the ride detail — removes it from W entirely (it is the same exclusion the scoring model honours). Pinning a slow ride off-scale would also hide *how* slow the yellow dot was, which is the one thing a 9-ride plot should show plainly. Removing the fence removes `quantile`, `FENCE_MIN_N`, `OFF_SCALE_Y`, the hollow/pinned point styles and their tests.
- **Y ticks**: step chosen from `[5,10,15,20,30,60,120,300,600,900]` s as the smallest that yields ≤ 5 ticks across `[yMin, yMax]`; ticks at multiples of the step inside the domain; labels via `fmt()`. The average label (`avg m:ss`) sits in the same gutter at `y(mean)`; any tick label within 12 px of it is dropped (label collision handled in the model, not the component).

### 3.5 X-axis: proportional to time within the window, fitted to the plot width — no panning

The original design panned a `PX_PER_DAY` strip through years of history. With the window fixed at 9 rides there is nothing to pan to: **the plot is a fixed-width View, no `ScrollView`, no scroll-to-end, no content-size dance.**

- `plotW` = measured width of the plot area (`onLayout`), minus the gutter. `x(t) = PAD_L + (t − tOldest) / (tNewest − tOldest) · (plotW − PAD_L − PAD_R)` with `PAD_L = PAD_R = 12`. Newest at the right edge, oldest at the left. With exactly one point (or identical timestamps) every point sits at `plotW − PAD_R`.
- x IS proportional to time (Nathan: "chronological"; a month off shows as a month of space). Consequence: nine rides in one week and one a year ago put eight dots in the right-hand tenth of the plot. That is the truth of the window and the caption/board disambiguate; points draw in chronological order so the newest is on top, and `hitSlop: 8` on each keeps them tappable. No minimum-separation nudging (it would be arithmetic that lies about time).
- **X ticks**: candidates are month boundaries inside `[tOldest, tNewest]` labelled `Sep`, with the first tick and every January carrying the year (`Jan 26`); when the whole span is under 45 days, Mondays labelled `dd Mon` instead. If more than `MAX_X_TICKS = 6` candidates, keep every `ceil(n / 6)`-th **counting back from the newest** so the most recent boundary is always labelled. Tick lines are hairlines in `t.cardBorder`; labels `t.textDim` 11 px just above the plot's bottom edge. Zero candidates (a span inside one month, ≥ 45 days is impossible, so this means < 45 days with no Monday — i.e. under 7 days): label the two ends with `towerDate(tOldest)` / `towerDate(tNewest)` instead.

### 3.6 Point colouring: purple = fastest of the window, green = faster than its average, yellow = slower

Nathan (Q3): *"Fastest is a purple dot. rest of the dots are either green/yellow for if they are above or below the average."* These are the app's tier colours applied to the scoring model's own pool, so §3.2's "no tier colours" (which is about re-judging ALL rides) does not apply here — the plot is precisely window-scoped.

- `tone: 'fastest' | 'faster' | 'slower'` per point, computed in the model: `'fastest'` = the minimum `timeS` in W (tie → the OLDEST of the tied, matching `towerModel`'s "first equal" PB rule; exactly one point is `'fastest'`); otherwise `timeS < meanS` → `'faster'`, else `'slower'`. No noise floor: with one ride the single dot is purple; with two, the other is yellow (it is ≥ the mean of the two). The colour is readable straight off the chart (above or below the drawn line), so it is a description of the picture, not a verdict needing `MIN_HISTORY`.
- Colours: `'fastest'` → `colors.purple`, r 5; `'faster'` → `colors.green`, r 4; `'slower'` → `YELLOW_TIER` (`tierColour.ts`), r 4. All filled. Never a local hex; never `colors.neutral` written directly (import `YELLOW_TIER` so the tier semantics stay in one place).
- **The purple dot is the window's fastest, NOT necessarily the all-time PB.** The board's ● is the all-time PB (`allTimeBestLapS`). They coincide only while the PB is among the last 9; after that the ● stays on the board and the plot's purple moves to the best of the current field. This is the point of the two visualisations (Nathan: "the tower shows absolute ranking … the scatterplot shows it in time"). The plot's hint line states it (§3.10).
- **Selected point** = an extra 2 px ring in `t.text` (ink), r 8. NOT an accent ring: `YELLOW_TIER === colors.neutral` is the same hue as the accent surface, so an accent ring on a yellow dot would vanish. The board row for the selected ride still gets the tower's accent LEFT BAR (identity chrome, as in `tower.tsx:223`).

### 3.7 Hand-rolled with plain Views — no `react-native-svg`

`react-native-svg` is a **native module**, and `README-dev.md:34` is explicit that any new native module forces an EAS dev-client rebuild on Nathan's phone. The shape inventory is trivial and smaller than before: ≤ 9 filled circles, one horizontal dotted line, ≤ 6 hairline ticks, text — every one a `View` with `borderRadius`/`borderWidth`, absolutely positioned from a pure layout model.

- **Dotted average line**: a `flexDirection: 'row'`, `overflow: 'hidden'` View of width `plotW` filled with `ceil(plotW / 6)` 2×2 px `borderRadius: 1` Views spaced 4 px, in `t.textDim`. Do **not** rely on `borderStyle: 'dotted'` on a single edge (Android draws it inconsistently; the repo's `chips.tsx` precedent is full borders only).
- **Tap**: every point is a `Pressable` with `hitSlop: 8`. Tapping selects it (state in the detail screen, not the plot): the point gets the ink ring, the matching board row gets the accent left bar, and a one-line **caption** appears directly under the plot: `Tue 05 Aug · 21:34 · P3 of 27 · open ›` (the position is the BOARD's all-time position for that ride, so the two surfaces are visibly the same ride). Tapping the caption opens the ride (`tabNav.openRide({ rideId, source: 'results', startedAtMs })`). Tapping a board row opens the ride directly (Strava: "tap any date … to be taken to that activity"). Tapping empty plot space clears the selection.
- **Sparse data**: 1 ranked ride → one purple dot at the right edge, the dotted line through it, `minSpan` gives it a readable axis; 0 ranked rides but some `not ranked`/`NO TIME` rides → the plot area shows the dim line `no ranked rides yet` at height 120 (not 220); the board still lists them. 0 rides at all cannot happen from the list (§3.8) but the detail must survive it (a ride deleted from a ride detail opened from here): same `no rides on file` line.

### 3.8 The list: ways with ≥ 1 stored result, most-ridden first

- Rows = every way in the catalog (`CATALOG.ways` — post-WP-3 this array holds the variants) with `storedResultsForWay(id).length ≥ 1`, sorted by that count **descending**, ties by most recent `startedAtMs` descending, then by label. Row: full label (`wayLabelIn(catalog, wayId)` — `"Home → Work · fast · via park"`, legacy `route:`-prefixed ids included, `isUserMintedWayId` handles both prefixes), right-aligned `N rides` (`1 ride`), second dim line `best m:ss · last Tue 05 Aug` (`allTimeBestLapS`, last result's date). Tap → `tabNav.openResults({ wayId })`.
- **Ways with zero rides are not rows.** RESULTS is about what you have been completing; unridden ways belong to ROUTES. A dim footer line states the count so nothing feels hidden: `4 more ways with no rides yet — see ROUTES` (omitted when 0).
- Empty state (no way has any result): one dim paragraph, `no results yet — finish a ride on a way and it shows up here`, no button.
- Header line above the list: `RESULTS` in `st.h2` style like RIDES/ROUTES. Once WP-1 lands, the sport badge line WP-1 B2 adds to ROUTES/RIDES goes here too (whichever WP lands second adds it — §9).
