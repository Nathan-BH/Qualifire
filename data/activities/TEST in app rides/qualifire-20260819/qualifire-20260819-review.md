## The three rides — what the GPX files actually show

### Ride 1 (morning, home → work, route B in the rain, chain incident)

**Your question — "does pause actually change the gpx data?" — has a two-part answer.**

**Part 1: the PAUSE button on today's build changes nothing, on purpose.** That's your own ruling from yesterday evening / this morning (cycle 020): PAUSE is an *accidental-stop guard*, not a real pause. Pressing it only opens a RESUME / END choice — the button itself says "recording continues · resume or end" — and recording and the lap clock keep running underneath. This was a deliberate consequence of your "luck counts, raw time is the truth" decision (D-042). So a pressed-and-resumed PAUSE leaves the GPX completely untouched: if you stand still, the file just fills with one point per second at the same spot.

**Part 2: and yet your file has a real hole in it — which means something other than pause happened.** The facts in the file:

- First point: 11:55:30, at home. Then **nothing for 18.4 minutes** — the next point is at 12:13:53, seven metres away. That is a true recording gap, not a cluster of standing-still points.
- The ride's internal name (baked in when START is pressed) is `20260819-121352` — i.e. **the recording session that produced this file was started at 12:13:52**, right where the gap ends. The single 11:55:30 point in front of it is almost certainly a stale cached GPS fix Android handed to the new session at startup. (The export file is *named* 1155 only because the export names files after the earliest point's clock time.)
- Later, mid-ride, you stood still twice — 12:19:32 (1.7 min) and 12:21:15 (4.5 min), about 2.4 km from home — and **those are fully recorded**: ~370 one-per-second points at the same spot. That's what a stop looks like in the current format. Recording never blinked there.
- Net: 41 minutes elapsed, of which ~18 min hole + ~6 min standing = roughly 16–17 minutes of actual riding for 6.0 km.

The most likely story: you pressed START around 11:55, the chain came off at the door, you pressed PAUSE and then — believing it was pause — **END** (they sit in the same menu), fixed the chain, and pressed START again at 12:13:52. If that's right, there should be a tiny stub ride from ~11:55 sitting in your RIDES tab — worth a look, it would confirm the story. The honest bottom line: **the file cannot tell us which buttons you pressed, and that is precisely the case for GPX+** (see point 1 below).

**The offset you saw is real and measured.** Your morning track runs a median of ~10 m from the drawn route-B line, 90% of points within ~19 m — a small, *consistent* sideways offset, exactly what you'd expect given that the morning route B reference is literally an evening ride reversed (that's what cycle 020 shipped: a single archived evening ride, flipped, explicitly flagged as a cold-start stand-in). Two things worth knowing before you re-reference:

- The offset costs you nothing in timing — the scoring corridor is 40 m wide and the "off route" paint threshold is 120 m. This is a cosmetics-and-honesty upgrade, not a fix for wrong times.
- Your ride *is* usable as the new reference geometry despite the hole: the gap is only 7 m wide on the ground, so the line itself is complete from home to work. Promoting it is exactly the still-open half of the "ratify the home→work rain route" item (B-60), and the mechanism you're asking for — "take this ride as the reference" — is designed but unbuilt (B-42). More under point 2.

### Ride 3 (evening, station → home in the rain, picked up route B near work)

- 8.8 km, 26 minutes, flawless 1-per-second recording, ends 10 m from the home landmark. One 1.3-minute stop ~500 m past work (light, probably).
- **Your account of the mid-ride "pick up" matches the file and the code exactly.** Your track passes within 50 m of the work landmark at 2.90 km (33% of the ride), and enters the evening-route-B corridor at 2.98 km. The engine's lock rule is "a candidate whose progress along its line reaches 400 m and leads every rival by 200 m wins" — and crucially, progress is counted **from wherever you first appear on its line**, not from its start. So a few hundred metres past work, evening route B legitimately won the lock and began firing gates. That mid-track lock is by design (it's also what lets the app recover and re-lock if it gets killed mid-ride).

One honest caveat: the engine currently locks once and can never unlock or re-detect (a known open gap, B-65) — so until your wet route is live, a ride like tonight's will keep half-becoming an evening-B ride.
- **Your wet route is genuinely a different route, not a variant of the existing one.** I measured tonight's track against the archived station→home "preferred" line you ratified in July: more than half of tonight's points are over 40 m away from it (median ~200 m — different streets for most of the middle). So "s>>h-w" should enter the catalog as a **second route on the station→home way**, sitting beside the preferred one — the model already supports exactly this (multiple routes per way, grouped, never colour-compared against each other — B-41) — rather than replacing it.

## Your five points, checked

### 1. "GPX+ is the next big implementation"

**Agreed, and you're pushing on a door the project already framed.** A full GPX+ spec has existed since 2026-08-16, written from your original idea: standard GPX plus an extensions block that Strava-type tools simply ignore, carrying — per file — when START was pressed, how long GPS took to first fix, which route locked and where, every gate detected (with its "estimated" flag), every GPS outage, every stop, storage errors, and per-point GPS accuracy. It was triaged then as "spec ahead of need — revisit after landmarks and ways exist." Landmarks and ways have existed since 2026-08-18. **The gate has cleared; nothing blocks building it.**

Two things I found this pass that sharpen it:

- **Half the hard part is already done without anyone telling you:** the recorder has been storing per-point GPS accuracy in the raw ride files on your phone all along (the plain GPX format just can't carry it, so the export drops it). The spec's open question "should we start storing accuracy before more rides accumulate without it?" is thus already answered in your favour — the missing piece is only the *event log* (button presses, lock, gates, outages) plus the export that stitches both in.
- **Today's ride 1 argues for one addition to the spec: log every button press verbatim.** The spec currently records "when START was pressed"; after this morning, it should record START / PAUSE / RESUME / END presses each with a timestamp. That single addition would have turned today's 20-minute detective story into one glance.

**Options:** (a) build GPX+ next cycle as specced plus the button-press log — small-to-medium, testable on your very next commute; (b) same, but staged: events-log-on-disk first (so data accrues immediately), export second; (c) fold in the spec's remaining open question — events inline in the ride file vs a sidecar file — which is a builder's call, not yours. My read: (b), because every ride ridden before the event log exists is a ride we can never explain.

### 2. Pick a reference ride per route, shortnames, draggable gates as percentages

Three separate things here, in three different states:

- **"Pick a single ride as THE reference" — designed, unbuilt.** The rule "ride 1 is the reference by default; promote any clean complete ride afterwards" has been on the books since the cold-start design (B-42), and "routes are ratified by you, never auto-discovered" is settled policy. What's never existed is any *interface* for it — every reference so far was promoted by you saying so in chat and a script doing it (that's literally how morning route B was minted this week: your words → a build script → the reversed evening ride became the reference). Your "copy the ref GPX into a folder and annotate it" matches how it already works internally; the missing piece is the button.
- **Shortnames — you've nearly reinvented the existing scheme, which is a good sign.** The catalog already names ways `home>work`, `station>home`, etc. Your `h>>w-w` adds two things: initials and a **condition suffix** (-w wet / -d dry). The initials are pure convenience — easy. The condition suffix needs one decision from you, because it can mean two different things: today, "wet" happens to mean *a physically different road* (the asphalt route), which the model handles as a separate route with its own name; but someday you'll ride the *same* road in the rain, and then "wet" is a property of the **ride**, not the route. Recommendation: shortnames name routes (h>>w-b, s>>h-wet as route names are fine), and weather becomes a tag on rides later — don't fuse them permanently.
- **Gates as percentages with draggable handles — the storage already supports it; the drag part has a written counter-argument.** Gates are stored as *metres along the route line* (e.g. morning route B's gates sit at 163 / 1802 / 3027 / 4352 / 5677 m of 5861 m), and gate sets are versioned so a move never silently corrupts history — middle-gate moves keep lap history, start/finish moves break it, and the design already prices that. Percent is just metres divided by length, so showing `G2 — 34% — 1,993 m` is trivial and your `G1-5%;G2-34%;G3-67%;G4-95%` notation is a perfectly good way for us to talk — I'd adopt it for communication *on top of* metres, not instead (5% of two different routes is two different distances, and metres are what the engine eats). One caution from the existing design work: dragging handles *on a phone map* was examined and argued against — your thumb covers the line and one pixel of a 4 km route is ~8 m — the proposed alternative being tap-a-gate-then-nudge (−50/−10/+10/+50 m buttons). On a **browser page with a mouse**, dragging is fine. Which leads to:

**The one build that answers all of point 2 (and point 5):** a route workbench page in the browser — one page per route showing the reference line on a real map, each gate as a draggable line with a live `% + metres + Google Maps link` readout, a "promote this ride to reference" picker, and a Save button that writes a file the team reads next cycle. This merges the gate-eyeball page that's been open since cycle 3 at zero build cost (B-31) with your reference-picker ask (B-42) and your percentage slider (point 5). It was offered in the last review too; it's still the highest-leverage small build in the queue.

### 3. The race-mode dot and line

- **The grey dot is not random — it's a signal.** The dot turns grey when you're more than 120 m from the drawn line, yellow when you're on it. On ride 3's first 3 km you were far from the drawn (preferred) line — grey, correctly. That said, your discomfort is legitimate on two counts: nobody ever told you the rule, and a *yellow* dot on a *yellow* route line is poor contrast even when everything is working. A distinct rider colour (with the off-route state as an outline change or badge, which already exists) is a fair ask.
- **The invisible gate circles in night mode — you're right, and I can confirm it in the code.** Gates ahead of you are deliberately *uncoloured* until their sector is scored (honesty rule: a gate must never wear a verdict colour it hasn't earned — that part should stay). But "uncoloured" was implemented as a transparent fill with a near-black ring — on the night basemap, that's near-black on near-black. In daylight theme the same rings show fine, which is exactly why you only noticed it at night. The honesty rule requires *no tier colour*, not *invisible*: a dim neutral ring that follows the theme is a 15-minute fix with no decision needed.
  - **Your follow-up (circles don't make sense, use a small line across the route instead) is a good call, and it's not a rival fix to the one above — the two combine.** A gate is stored as a single chainage (metres along the line), and the engine already knows the route's heading at that point, so drawing "a short tick perpendicular to the route, centred on the gate" is no harder than drawing a circle — it's the same data, a different two-line drawing routine. It also reads better than a circle: a line across the road matches how a finish line actually looks, it can't be mistaken for a rider or a landmark pin, and it doesn't need a radius that has to stay legible at every zoom level the way a circle does. It still needs a colour, though — a thin line in the same near-black tone would go invisible on the night map for the same reason the circle did, so this is a shape fix, not a substitute for the theme-aware-colour fix above. Recommend doing both together: line-across-the-route shape, dim theme-aware colour until the sector scores.
- **Dotted-ahead / solid-behind — genuinely new.** I searched the code, the ideas file, the decisions log and the design docs: nobody has proposed or built it. It's technically clean (the engine knows your position along the route at every second, so splitting the line into "ridden" and "still to come" is cheap, and dashed lines are a native map-style feature). I also think your framing argument is the strong one: a dotted line ahead reads as *suggestion*, which becomes load-bearing the moment route alternatives exist. Speaking of which —
- **Your "route1/route2 differ in one street" sub-idea already exists in the model:** multiple routes per way, grouped, auto-matched at lock, never colour-compared against each other (B-41, unbuilt). What's new in your version is the *rendering* — dotted branches that split, with only the taken branch drawn solid afterwards. File that as the display layer of the alternates work, not a separate feature.

### 4. "Gates scattered on my usual roads instead of routes"

This is the most interesting idea in your notes, and it half-exists in the project's thinking already — worth knowing before deciding to prototype:

- The routing/segmentation design already commits to **per-sector comparison rather than per-route**: "the comparison set is per sector, not per route... sector 2 may have 40 comparable rides while sectors 1, 3 and 4 have zero. That is a legitimate, honest board." And the data model deliberately parked "sector sharing across ways" as tempting-but-not-yet (it turns sector identity into a web instead of a simple list). Your gate-field idea is the logical endpoint of that thread: drop the route as the primary object entirely, keep only gates that activate as you pass.
- The honest engineering counterpoint, so you have it: today a gate is not a point — it's a position *on a route line*, and crossing detection works by projecting your GPS onto that line. A free-floating gate needs its own little crossing line and direction to fire cleanly (otherwise passing a gate on a parallel street, or in the wrong direction, fires it falsely). And a time between two gates is only comparable when the road *between* them was the same — which is the very thing the route concept guarantees and a pure gate-field doesn't. So the likely landing zone isn't "gates instead of routes" but "gates shared *across* routes" — which is where the project's own parked idea was already pointing.
- **On testing it side by side: yes, structurally cheap.** The live engine is a self-contained unit fed one GPS fix per second; demos and tests already spin up their own instances. A second, experimental "gate-field engine" could run beside the real one on the same fixes, both logging to GPX+, without touching the existing framework — exactly the shape you asked for. One correction to the plan though: **you rode today on the standalone Preview app, which freezes its code at build time** — quick-refresh experimenting is what the dev client (phone + PC on the same WiFi) is for. Side-by-side on the Preview app means rebuilding it each iteration (~15 min a pop); side-by-side on the dev client is instant but needs Metro running.
- **The cheapest first step needs no phone at all:** replay the 624-ride archive against a gate-field model offline and see what it would have produced — the replay machinery exists and is proven. That answers "is this idea good?" with evidence before anyone builds live UI. I'd do that before any on-bike experiment.

### 5. Talking about points on a route as percentages

- Nothing like a percentage slider exists yet — but the underlying quantity does: everything in the engine is already "metres along the route line" (chainage), and percent is just that number divided by route length. So this is a *display and communication* feature, not a data-model change — and it belongs on the same workbench page as point 2 (a slider you drag, showing `42.3% = 3,687 m` plus a Google Maps link, that you can then quote to Claude verbatim).
- One caveat to carry: if a route's reference line is ever *replaced* (which is exactly what you proposed for morning route B in ride 1!), its length changes and every percentage shifts meaning slightly. The gate-set versioning already handles this for gates; quoted percentages in old chat messages just need to be read against the reference that existed at the time.
- **Your train-tracks example has a better answer than a slider:** there's an open, designed, zero-phone-build item to extract traffic signals per route from OpenStreetMap so that "stop at a known signal = luck" becomes computable (B-33). Level crossings live in the same map data and can be pulled the same way — meaning the two railway crossings on your dry route could be located and named *automatically*, no dragging needed. The slider is still worth having for everything that isn't in the map data.

## Where you're right, the nuances, and the one real correction

- **Right:** GPX+ is the unlock and it's ready to build (spec exists, blocker cleared); the reference-promotion flow you want is designed but has no interface; your shortname style matches the existing way names; the invisible night gates are a genuine rendering oversight, and your line-across-the-route shape is a real improvement on top of the colour fix; the grey/yellow dot needs a rethink (or at least an explanation); dotted-ahead is new and good; ride 3's mid-ride pickup is real, explained, and self-curing once the route goes live; your wet station→home ride is genuinely a new route and should be ratified as one.
- **Nuance:** "wet/dry" should name *routes* only while wet actually means a different road; the drag-handles idea is right for the browser, argued-against for the phone; the gate-field idea most likely lands as "gates shared across routes" rather than "no routes", and deserves an offline archive replay before any live prototype; the gate marker shape (circle vs. line) and its colour are two independent fixes that should ship together.
- **The correction:** the pause button did not, and could not, trim your GPX — by your own D-042 ruling it never stops recording. The 18-minute hole in ride 1 means recording was actually *off*: almost certainly a START ~11:55 that got ENDed during the chain incident (PAUSE and END share a menu), then a fresh START at 12:13:52. Check the RIDES tab for a stub ride from ~11:55 to confirm. Until GPX+ exists, that diagnosis is deduction, not data — which is your point 1, proven by your point 1's absence.

**Suggested picks, if you want a shortlist:** (1) GPX+ with button-press logging — it retroactively explains every future weirdness; (2) the browser route workbench — one page that covers reference-picking, gate handles with %, and the percentage slider in a single build; (3) the small render fixes bundled into whichever cycle runs next: gate markers redrawn as a line-across-the-route in a dim theme-aware colour (instead of the near-invisible circle), and a one-line "grey dot = off the drawn line" hint somewhere you'd see it. The gate-field experiment I'd start as an offline archive replay, not an app change.
