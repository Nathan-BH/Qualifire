## Anomalies / needs your attention

1. **Ride 3's file contradicts your missed-gate account.** You wrote: took a slightly different route near work, missed the second gate, third gate picked up, sector 4 clocked, and "at the end it estimated a complete ride time because one sector was missing." The file says: **all five gates fired as real crossings (`estimated="false"`), evenly spaced at 3.4% / 25.6% / 49.9% / 74.5% / 96.8% of the ridden track, no estimation anywhere.** Meanwhile **ride 1's file shows exactly the behaviour you describe**: gates 1–3 marked `estimated="true"` in a 0.6-second burst when you rejoined the route near the church, real gate 4, and an end-of-ride lock. My best reconciliation: the "estimated complete ride time" you saw belongs to **ride 1's summary screen**, and ride 3's "missed gate" impression came from the gates being invisible on the live map until crossed (your own other observation). **Please check both ride summaries in the app**: if ride 3's summary really does show an estimated sector, we have a UI-vs-export mismatch bug, which is worse than either of us thinking.
2. **Ride 2 was not unlogged — it was mislogged.** You wrote "this route did not exist so I rode without proper logging I think." The file shows the engine **locked onto `ChurchFosh` at 12:22:21, 402 m into that route's line, and fired two real gates** (gate0 12:20:52, gate1 12:23:11) before you diverged toward the station and it went quiet. That's the lock-once-never-unlock gap (flagged on the 19th as B-65) producing its second wrong-route ride record in five days: church→station shares its first kilometre with church→fosh, so the impostor won again. Until unlock/re-detect exists, every ride on an uncatalogued route that starts along a catalogued one will half-become that other ride.
3. **Ride 1's lock fired at the END press.** The `HomeChurch` lock is stamped 10:29:14.018 — between your PAUSE (10:29:04.4) and END (10:29:14.5), at chainage 6,244 m, i.e. essentially the last second of the ride. Related: in both ride 1 and ride 2, **gate events carry timestamps earlier than the lock event** (ride 1's gate0 fired 18 minutes before its lock). I can't tell from the files whether that's live pre-lock gate tracking on the selected/leading candidate, or back-filling at lock time. Not necessarily a bug — but the semantics should be pinned down in the GPX+ spec, because right now the event ordering can't be read literally.
4. **`firstFixDelayS` goes negative.** Ride 1: −7.489 s; ride 3: −11.052 s. Cause: the first "fix" in the file predates the START press — it's the stale cached position Android hands the session at startup (the same phenomenon diagnosed on the 19th, now measurable). The metric as defined ("time from START to first fix") silently becomes "how stale was the cached fix" when negative. Small spec decision needed: either clamp at 0 and log the stale fix separately, or rename the field's meaning.
5. **The export keeps garbage points.** Ride 1's second track point (10:10:01, accuracy **500 m**) sits ~220 m off your true position at 50.8372982, 4.6411954 ([map](https://maps.google.com/?q=50.8372982,4.6411954)) — any standard GPX viewer will draw a spike there, and a naive speed calculation reads 43–47 km/h moments you never rode (your real day-maximum was 39.7 km/h, ride 3). GPX+'s per-point `qf:acc` is precisely the cure — but only for tools that read it. Worth deciding: should the export drop (or flag) points above some accuracy threshold, or is that the viewer's job? (This matters directly for your GPX-viewer idea, and for promoting ride 1 as a reference — see below.)
6. Trivial but for the record: your notes say "Ride 2 at 12:07"; the button log says START was pressed at **12:17:41** (file correctly named 1217). Presumably a typo.

### 2. A proper GPX viewer, workbench-style, with auto-computed default gates

**Strongly agreed, and today's data both sharpens and slightly complicates your default-gates proposal.**

- The need is real and now urgent for a new reason: GPX+ files carry an event layer no generic tool (Strava et al.) will ever show. A viewer that draws the yellow track **plus** the qf events — button presses, lock point, real gates in one style and estimated gates in another, stops, outages, per-point accuracy as line opacity — would have answered every question in your notes in one glance, no Claude pass required. The event layer is the argument for building our own rather than adopting someone else's.
- Your defaults (start/end at 1% / 99.5%, middle gates at 25 / 50 / 75%) are close to what the engine already produces: tonight's real gates sat at 3.4 / 25.6 / 49.9 / 74.5 / 96.8% of your ridden distance — the middles are dead on; only the endpoints differ (the engine's start/finish gates sit further inside than 1% / 99.5%, roughly 3% from each end tonight). Concretely, on ride 1 your proposed defaults would fall at: 1% → 50.836158, 4.639300 ([map](https://maps.google.com/?q=50.836158,4.639300)); 25% → 50.846756, 4.654993 ([map](https://maps.google.com/?q=50.846756,4.654993)); 50% → 50.854648, 4.663450 ([map](https://maps.google.com/?q=50.854648,4.663450)); 75% → 50.860168, 4.683209 ([map](https://maps.google.com/?q=50.860168,4.683209)); 99.5% → 50.857810, 4.697631 ([map](https://maps.google.com/?q=50.857810,4.697631)). Click those five links and judge your own defaults on a real ride — that's the viewer experience, done by hand.
- Two cautions carried over from the 19th's analysis, both still true: **percent-of-ride ≠ percent-of-route** (your ridden 7.12 km vs the ~6.3 km reference line means the same percentage lands in different places — the viewer should say which one it's showing), and **raw tracks contain junk** (ride 1's 500 m-accuracy spike would visibly distort a naive viewer's line and its length-based percentages — the viewer must filter on `qf:acc`, which conveniently only GPX+ files can offer).
- Placement: this is the ride-side sibling of the browser **route workbench** argued for in the last review (the B-31 + B-42 merge: reference line, draggable gates with % + metres readouts, promote-to-reference picker). Same map component, same chainage math, same percent readouts — one page with a "ride" mode and a "route" mode, or two pages sharing everything, but designed together. Building the GPX viewer alone and the workbench later as a stranger would waste most of the overlap.
