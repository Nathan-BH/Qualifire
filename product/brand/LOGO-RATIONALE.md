# Logo concepts — design rationale

Designer · 2026-08-15 · Companion to `BRAND.md` (read that first: the story the
marks must serve). Every claim here should trace to a recorded decision or a
BRAND principle; where a concept's story was weak, the design was changed, not
the story stretched.

Shared ground rules, from BRAND: charcoal ground (paddock `#17171b`), warm ink,
signature yellow `#F5C542` (which *is* the neutral "time posted" tier — the
brand colour is deliberately the app's default temperature, not its rarest
prize). No red. No bicycles, wings, speedometers: Qualifire's one proprietary
visual idea is the **gate — a line crossed at speed** (D-011), and the marks
are built from it.

---

## 1 · gate_q — the icon-weight mark

**The idea.** A closed ring — the lap, the circuit, the same road every day —
crossed by one yellow line. In the app, a gate is always drawn perpendicular
across the route (Setup screen, D-011); here the gate crosses the ring
perpendicular to its local direction, at 4–5 o'clock, where a Q's tail lives.

**Why it makes sense for Qualifire.** The whole product is this gesture: a
line only the app can see, laid across an ordinary path, is what turns a
circle into a Q — exactly the way the start gate turns a commute into a lap.
One shape, the entire pitch.

**Honest critique + what was changed.** The first draft attached the tail
*outside* the ring — it read as a magnifying glass (the search-icon trap).
Fixed: the tail now crosses the ring inside-to-outside, which no search glyph
does, and which is also the truthful geometry — gates cross the route, they
don't hang off it.

**Role.** Launcher icon / favicon weight of the family. Not a standalone
identity — it is concept 5 with the wordmark removed.

## 2 · sector_strip — the instrument mark

**The idea.** The live screen's progress strip, verbatim: four slots, sectors
completed so far. Three unlit outlines, the fourth filled yellow — the moment
a time posts and the earcon fires.

**Why it makes sense.** It is the only mark drawn from what the rider actually
sees mid-ride, and it encodes the tier ladder's non-colour half (filled >
outlined — D-007's redundancy rule) in the logo itself. Yellow-not-green-not-
purple is deliberate: the brand celebrates the *ordinary* posted time; purple
is not for chrome (BRAND P2).

**Honest critique + what was changed.** The draft made the lit chip taller
than its neighbours — pure flair with no referent (the real strip never grows
a chip). Removed; all four slots are now equal, lit = filled, which is the
app's actual grammar. Remaining weakness, stated plainly: at 48 dp it can read
as a generic progress indicator; it depends on the app's own iconography
being learned first. Strong as an in-app empty-state / splash motif, weak as
a cold-start launcher icon.

## 3 · lap_ring — the aspiration mark

**The idea.** The lap as a ring of four sector arcs, each wearing a tier:
two yellow, one green, one purple — with the gap and ink tick at 12 o'clock
as the start gate, the only place the ring is open (a flying lap starts and
ends at a line, not a point).

**Why it makes sense.** It is the *perfect day* — the board you'd get if every
sector lit. The tier trio is the app's entire colour vocabulary in one glyph,
and purple occupying exactly one arc of four keeps D-008's proportions
honest-ish (purple is rare) rather than painting the whole ring in jackpot.

**Honest critique, stated rather than designed away.** This mark permanently
displays a state the app almost never shows — BRAND P2 says colour is earned,
and a logo can't earn it. That's defensible as aspiration (every team's livery
promises the podium), but it *is* the loosest fit to the brand's restraint
principle of the five. If Nathan's instinct is restraint, this one dies first.

## 4 · flying_start — the physics mark

**The idea.** A timing diagram of the app's central measurement: the yellow
gate line vertical; two charcoal streaks (unmeasured motion, before and
after); one ink streak with the rider-dot just *past* the line. The moment
depicted is D-011's crossing timestamp — interpolated between two GPS fixes
at 20–26 km/h, the most precise thing Qualifire does.

**Why it makes sense.** Rolling start made visible: the dot does not launch
from the line, it is already at speed when the line happens to it. That is the
product's answer to "when does timing start" (this session's flying-lap
discussion) drawn as a mark.

**Honest critique.** Most conceptually pure, least nameable — at small size it
can read as sliders/equalizer, and nothing in it says "Q". Best as the
animated splash (streaks sweep, dot crosses, buzz fires) rather than the
static identity.

## 5 · monogram_wordmark — the identity (Nathan's current pick)

**The idea.** Concept 1's argument carried to the full lockup: the ink ring is
the lap; the yellow stroke crossing it is the gate; ring + gate = Q, the
initial of the qualifying lap the app is named for. Below, QUALIFIRE set
small, heavy, uppercase, letterspaced — the timing-tower typography of
BRAND P3, where numbers are heroes and words are servants.

**Why the yellow tail specifically.** The gate is the only coloured element,
and it wears the signature/neutral yellow — "time posted". The mark thus makes
the brand's quietest promise, not its loudest: cross the line and your time
counts. Green and purple stay out of the logo entirely, reserved for earned
surfaces (BRAND P2) — which is exactly why the rare purple day will feel like
something the *app* did, not something the letterhead spoiled.

**Honest critique + what was changed.** Draft had two flaws: the tail hung
outside the ring (magnifier again) and a floating yellow tick sat inside the
ring meaning nothing. Both fixed — one yellow stroke, crossing the ring,
carrying the whole story. Remaining trade-off: with the wordmark it needs
vertical space; at launcher size, drop the wordmark and it *is* concept 1.
That's the family logic: **5 is the identity, 1 is its launcher form** —
adopting 5 costs nothing extra to get a coherent icon.

---

## Recommendation

Adopt **5 + 1 as one family** (identity + launcher form). Keep **2** as an
in-app motif (splash, empty states) where its meaning is self-teaching. Park
**4** as a future animated-splash storyboard. Retire **3** unless the
aspiration argument outweighs the restraint principle — Nathan's call.
